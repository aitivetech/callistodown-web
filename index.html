The game

A survival game where the physics is the game.

Callisto Down is survival and building on Callisto, the most heavily cratered surface in the solar system. Air, heat, water and power are things you build, and you can see them work. Structures carry loads, hold pressure, leak and collapse. Gravity, temperatures, the length of the day and the pressure of the water are Callisto's real numbers.

Build a base on the surface. Power a melt drill and bore through about a kilometre of ice. Ride the cage down, launch a submarine and explore a subglacial ocean stacked in ten biomes, each stranger than the last. Better tech takes you deeper.

01 · Build

A base that has to work

Nothing keeps you alive by default. Sealed habitats track their pressure, oxygen, CO₂ and temperature. Heat leaks through the walls you chose. A compact reactor becomes the hearth of the base, and its radiation follows the shielding you put around it.

The sun is a weak star at 5.2 AU, and the night lasts eight Earth days. Prepare for it, or freeze.

02 · Descend

Melt through a kilometre of ice

The melt-drill rig turns reactor power into a hot head that sinks through the ice shell. Meltwater is pumped up and vents at the collar as a plume that boils and freezes in vacuum. When the head breaks into the cavern below, a winch cage carries you down the shaft.

03 · Navigate

Jupiter never moves

Callisto is tidally locked, so Jupiter hangs fixed in the sky, eight times the width of the full Moon. Its phases follow the local time, and through the long night its light shows you the land in grey. It is your landmark, your clock and your compass.

04 · See

Ray-traced light, placed by hand

Global illumination is ray traced. Light passes tinted through ice and glass, and water absorbs it the way the substance does: red dies within metres. Under the ice there is no sun and no sky. Every photon comes from your lamps, your floodlights, a vent or something alive.

  • Real physics, real units

    Callisto's 1.236 m/s² for bodies, vehicles and you. Materials with real density, strength, conductivity and gas tightness. Water and air move between compartments under real pressure.

  • Dig it, build it

    A voxel world at 25 cm resolution. Dig and cut with your tools, harvest what the ground is made of and build blocks and modules. Unsupported structures fall, and debris keeps its materials.

  • Co-op

    Play with friends on a listen server or a dedicated server. The simulation is server-authoritative: every player sees the same world, the same heat, the same water.

The Ocean Stack

Ten bands down

Under the ice lies a funnel of water five kilometres deep, its walls stepped in ten bands. Each one has its own place, its own physics and its own light. Your hull decides how far down you can go.

  1. B10–150 m≤ 2 bar

    The Rime Vault

    The arrival domes and the underside of the ice: marine-ice ceilings, brinicle forests and lamplight caught in platelet ice.

  2. B2150–450 m≤ 6 bar

    The Snowfall Terraces

    Broad terraces under constant marine snow, with white carbonate chimney fields. Falling snow turns floodlights into solid beams.

  3. B3450–800 m≤ 10.5 bar

    The Mirror Veils

    Sharp layers of brine that reflect like mirrors from below, internal waves rolling along them, and slow underwater brine falls.

  4. Minnow limit · 10 bar
  5. B4800–1200 m≤ 16 bar

    The Ember Vents

    Black smokers and sulfide mounds up to 600 K at the throats. Amber light, spark columns and heat shimmer. The first band where heat is free.

  6. B51200–1700 m≤ 22 bar

    The Clathrate Galleries

    Walls and organ pipes of translucent gas hydrate, stable only at this pressure. Lamp heat makes it fizz into silver bubble streams.

  7. B61700–2300 m≤ 30 bar

    The Lodestone Reefs

    Magnetite reefs striped with glowing life along the field lines. The glow rises and falls with Jupiter's 10.2-hour sweep of the conductive ocean.

  8. Narwhal limit · 30 bar
  9. B72300–3000 m≤ 39 bar

    The Opal Cathedrals

    Columns and curtains of silica sinter hundreds of metres tall. The sinter pipes light: flood one column and it glows from within.

  10. B83000–3700 m≤ 48 bar

    The Hollow

    The funnel opens into a void. The walls are out of lamp range. Navigate by sonar, the depth gauge and memory, while walls of light drift past far away.

  11. B93700–4400 m≤ 58 bar

    The Lattice Floor

    The walls turn too regular: hexagonal columns, lattices in exact repeats, fractures that run straight. The water is warmer than it should be.

  12. Deep-hull limit · 60 bar
  13. B104400–5000 m≤ 65 bar

    The Root Threshold

    The bottom of the funnel, about 800 m across. What waits here is still being designed.

Depths are below the ice ceiling, pressures at each band's floor. Band names are working names. The ten bands are in the world now; their dangers come later.

Development status

In development

Callisto Down is an early, playable work in progress, built on our own engine. There is no release date and no price yet. Builds are for following along and testing.

Playable now

  • The cratered surface around the start site, with Callisto's gravity, day, night and temperatures
  • Digging, harvesting, crafting, research and building
  • Habitats with air, heat and pressure; a reactor and power
  • The melt drill and the winch cage down to the ocean
  • The Minnow submarine in the upper ocean
  • All ten Ocean Stack bands in the world
  • Co-op on a listen or dedicated server

Being worked on

  • Deeper hulls and deeper bases for the lower bands
  • The dangers of each band
  • Story and quests
  • Visual polish and lighting
  • macOS
